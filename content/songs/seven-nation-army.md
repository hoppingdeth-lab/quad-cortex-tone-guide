sound: >-
  The main riff should sound like a guitar pushed into bass-register
  territory: a strong, clear note one octave below the played pitch,
  with a lightly driven, mid-forward amp tone. The octave effect is
  the defining part of the sound, so set it fully wet and keep the
  fuzz bypassed for the riff. For the slide solo, bypass the octave
  effect and engage the Big Muff-style fuzz for a rough, sustained
  sound. Keep the response raw and direct rather than adding extra
  ambience or polish.

beginner_route: >-
  Tune to open A, then place Wham at the start of the chain and set
  it one octave down with a fully wet output. Follow it with the
  Bassman-style amp and 4x10 cabinet. Leave Fuzz Pi bypassed for the
  main riff. If you want the slide solo, bypass Wham and engage
  Fuzz Pi. Use a slide and listen for clear low notes that retain
  their attack rather than turning into an indistinct rumble.

veteran_notes: >-
  Jack White used a Kay hollowbody guitar tuned to open A and a
  DigiTech Whammy set one octave down for the signature riff. A
  Guitar World gear breakdown identifies the guitar as an early
  1960s Kay K6533 archtop with a single neck pickup, and reports a
  mid-1960s Sears Silvertone 1485 amp with a 6x10 cabinet loaded with
  Jensen C10Q speakers. It also reports a Big Muff Pi for the noisy
  slide solo. The specific equipment and settings are published
  gear-analysis details; they are not all confirmed in a direct
  recording-session account from White.

  Neural DSP's official device list includes Wham, based on the
  DigiTech Whammy, and Fuzz Pi, based on the Electro-Harmonix Big
  Muff Pi. The list does not include a Silvertone 1485 model or a
  6x10 guitar cabinet. US Tweed Basslad Bright, based on a Fender
  Bassman Tweed, is used here as a broad vintage American amp
  substitute. 410 US Basslad PR10 is a Bassman-style 4x10 with
  Jensen P10R speakers. Its speaker count and model differ from the
  reported Silvertone 6x10 and Jensen C10Q combination.

  The chain is serial: Wham, optional Fuzz Pi, amp and cabinet.
  Fuzz Pi is placed after Wham to match the published account of
  the solo effect order. For the main riff, leave Fuzz Pi bypassed.
  For the slide solo, bypass Wham and engage Fuzz Pi; this gives
  you a practical solo scene without implying that the octave-down
  effect remains active.

before_you_build: >-
  Tune E-A-E-A-C#-E from the lowest string to the highest. Open A
  supports the slide phrasing and is the tuning reported for this
  part. If you use a different tuning, the pitch-shifted riff may
  still sound low, but the chord and slide voicings will differ.

  A Kay-style hollowbody with a single-coil neck pickup is a useful
  starting point. If you use a different guitar, try the neck pickup
  and reduce excess treble before increasing bass. The octave-down
  effect needs clean, stable notes at its input, so check your tuning
  and intonation first.

  Use a slide for the chord accents and solo. Keep the guitar volume
  high enough to drive the amp consistently, and use a controlled
  picking attack so Wham can track the notes clearly.

build_intro: >-
  These settings are practical starting points, not verified studio
  control positions. The Quad Cortex models approximate the original
  Whammy, Big Muff, Silvertone amp and 6x10 cabinet. Set the octave
  effect first, then balance the amp and cabinet around its low
  output.

build_steps:
  - title: "Tune and prepare the guitar"
    description: >-
      Tune to open A (E-A-E-A-C#-E). Use a hollowbody guitar and
      neck pickup if available, then check intonation and tuning
      stability. Set up a slide for the chord accents and solo.
      Begin with a firm, even attack so the octave effect receives
      a clear signal.

  - title: "Set the octave-down effect"
    description: >-
      Add Wham at the start of the chain. Select the one-octave-down
      setting, or set the shift to -12 semitones if the controls are
      presented numerically. Set the effect fully wet, around 100
      percent mix if available. The main riff should sound as a
      single shifted note, without an obvious unshifted guitar
      underneath.

  - title: "Set the vintage amp approximation"
    description: >-
      Select US Tweed Basslad Bright. Start with Gain or Volume
      around 4.5/10, Bass 5/10, Mid 5/10 and Treble 6/10 where those
      controls are available. The amp should be just at the edge of
      breakup: clear enough for the riff's note changes, but with
      some roughness when you dig in. These are suggested values
      for the Quad Cortex model, not Silvertone settings.

  - title: "Choose the cabinet"
    description: >-
      Select 410 US Basslad PR10. If microphone choices are
      available, begin with a dynamic microphone slightly off the
      speaker centre. Keep the low cut off or very low; if a cut is
      needed, try around 35-40 Hz so the octave-shifted notes retain
      their fundamental. Try a high cut near 7 kHz if the tone is
      fizzy. These are starting points for the substitute cabinet,
      not the original mic setup.

  - title: "Set up the Big Muff-style solo sound"
    description: >-
      Keep Fuzz Pi after Wham in the chain. For the distorted slide
      solo, bypass Wham and engage Fuzz Pi. Start with Sustain around
      7/10, Tone around 6/10 and Volume at unity, then adjust by ear.
      Increase Sustain if the slide notes need more hold, and lower
      Tone if the upper harmonics become harsh. Treat these as
      practical Quad Cortex starting points rather than verified
      pedal settings.

  - title: "Balance the scenes"
    description: >-
      Level-match the octave riff, slide chords and fuzz solo. The
      riff may feel louder because its low notes carry strongly;
      reduce its scene output if it dominates the mix. Raise the solo
      scene slightly only if it needs to cut through. Avoid adding
      more bass before checking the cabinet low cut and overall
      output level.

scenes:
  - id: "A"
    name: "Octave Riff"
    description: >-
      Engage Wham at one octave down with a fully wet output.
      Bypass Fuzz Pi. Use the amp and cabinet at the suggested
      starting settings. Keep the notes distinct and the low end
      controlled.

  - id: "B"
    name: "Slide Chords"
    description: >-
      Bypass Wham and Fuzz Pi. Keep the amp just at the edge of
      breakup for jangly, mildly overdriven slide chords. Adjust
      guitar volume or amp gain slightly if the chords need more
      grit.

  - id: "C"
    name: "Fuzz Slide Solo"
    description: >-
      Bypass Wham and engage Fuzz Pi after it in the serial chain.
      Use the slide for sustained, noisy phrases. Raise the scene
      output slightly if the solo needs more presence, while keeping
      the fuzz from overwhelming the note shape.

troubleshooting:
  - problem: "The octave-down notes glitch or drop out?"
    solution: >-
      Check tuning and intonation, use the neck pickup and play
      cleanly into Wham. Reduce excess signal processing before the
      pitch block and confirm that its output is set fully wet for
      the main riff.

  - problem: "The riff sounds too muddy?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Bass slightly on the amp, check that the cabinet low
      cut is not set too high, and lower the guitar volume only if
      the amp is being overdriven too heavily. Preserve the low
      fundamentals that give the riff its bass-like weight.

  - problem: "The riff does not sound low enough?"
    solution: >-
      Confirm that Wham is set to one octave down rather than an
      octave-up or harmony mode. Check for an unshifted dry signal
      in the mix control and set the effect fully wet.

  - problem: "The slide solo is too smooth or quiet?"
    solution: >-
      Engage Fuzz Pi, raise Sustain gradually and check its output
      level against the riff scene. Use stronger slide pressure and
      a firmer attack for more bite, then reduce Tone if the added
      harmonics become piercing.
