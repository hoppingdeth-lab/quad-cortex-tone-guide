sound: >-
  Build a thick, dark high-gain sound with enough midrange to keep the
  riff present and enough definition to hear the low-string attack.
  The low C should feel heavy but controlled, while chord accents and
  longer notes retain sustain. This recipe targets Carpenter's amplified
  rhythm part rather than every guitar and studio texture on the record.
  A restrained Flangerish setting can add slow movement to evoke the
  swirling guitar character in the song.

beginner_route: >-
  Tune to Drop C and start with Brit 2203 into 412 Brit 60A G75 80s.
  Set the amp for a dense crunch, then raise its gain until the riff
  sustains without losing note definition. Add Green 808 only if the
  low notes need more focus. Use Simple Gate only if noise between
  phrases is a problem. Then try Flangerish at a slow rate and low depth
  if a subtle swirl helps evoke the song's guitar character.

veteran_notes: >-
  In a 2000 Total Guitar interview, republished by Guitar World,
  Carpenter described his main amps as Marshall and named his ESP
  guitars, TC Electronic FireworX and Rocktron Replifex rack effects.
  In a separate 2000 interview about White Pony, he recalled tracking
  nearly all of his guitar parts through a Marshall JMP-1 preamp and
  doubling/panning the main guitar part. This is useful recording-era
  context, not proof of the exact Knife Prty signal path or settings.
  He later said that he used a standard-scale seven-string throughout
  White Pony, set up like his six-strings with an extra high-E string
  on top as a drone.

  TuneLit identifies Drop C for the White Pony album. The recipe gives
  the six-string tuning as C-G-C-F-A-D and notes Carpenter's additional
  high E. His precise guitar model and pickup choice for Knife Prty,
  the exact Marshall model, cabinet, microphone placement and any
  effects used on the track are not confirmed here. Chino Moreno did
  describe a "crazy swirl" on Stephen's guitar on Knife Prty. Flangerish
  is therefore included as an optional, ear-led way to approximate that
  documented character, not as a claim about the original effect or its
  placement.

  Brit 2203 is Neural DSP's JCM800 model. It is a suitable stock
  Marshall-family starting point. The TC FireworX and Rocktron Replifex were
  era equipment; no particular Quad Cortex effects have been added to
  imitate them without track-specific evidence.

  Green 808 is offered as an optional modern boost, not as a historical
  Carpenter effect. Simple Gate is practical noise control. Flangerish
  is a practical modulation approximation rather than a verified part
  of the session chain. None of these optional blocks is required to
  make the core amp-and-cab tone.

before_you_build: >-
  This is an original, ear-led Quad Cortex recipe inspired by Stephen
  Carpenter's guitar sound on the original 2000 studio recording. It is
  not an exact reconstruction of the session chain or a supplied
  preset file.

  Tune a seven-string to C-G-C-F-A-D-E, low to high, if you want to
  include Carpenter's extra high-E drone string. A six-string in
  C-G-C-F-A-D will cover the main six-string range. Use a suitable
  string gauge and check intonation after tuning down to Drop C.
  Begin with the bridge humbucker and the guitar volume fully open.

build_intro: >-
  These are practical starting points, not verified recording-session
  settings. Adjust for your guitar, string gauge, pickups, monitoring
  and playing level. Set the amp and cabinet sound first; add the
  optional boost only if it improves the low-string response, then try
  Flangerish only if it adds useful movement without blurring the riff.
  The Scenes below are practical performance proposals, not verified

build_steps:
  - title: "Tune and prepare the guitar"
    description: >-
      Tune the six main strings to C-G-C-F-A-D. If using a seven-string
      configured like Carpenter's White Pony instrument, add the high E
      string above them. Select the bridge humbucker and check that the
      low C remains firm and intonated. Use deliberate muting so the
      low string stops cleanly between phrases.

  - title: "Set the Brit 2203"
    description: >-
      Choose Brit 2203. As approximate starting points, try Gain around
      6/10, Bass 4.5/10, Mid 5.5/10, Treble 5/10 and Presence 4/10.
      Set the master and output for the required level. These are
      suggested positions for this model, not historical settings.
      Raise Gain gradually until notes sustain, backing off if the
      low C becomes indistinct.

  - title: "Choose the cabinet"
    description: >-
      Place 412 Brit 60A G75 80s after the amp. If microphone controls
      are available, begin with a dynamic microphone slightly off the
      speaker centre. Try a low cut around 70-90 Hz and a high cut near
      8 kHz as starting points. These are mix-shaping suggestions, not
      verified microphone or EQ settings from the session.

  - title: "Add the optional Green 808"
    description: >-
      If the low notes feel loose, put Green 808 before Brit 2203.
      Start with Drive around 1.5/10, Tone around 4.5/10 and Level
      around 7/10. These approximate settings use the overdrive as a
      boost, not as the main distortion source. Bypass it if the sound
      becomes too compressed or loses its low-end weight.

  - title: "Set the optional gate"
    description: >-
      Enable Simple Gate before the overdrive and amp only if the rig
      is noisy. Set the threshold just high enough to quiet pauses.
      Check that sustained notes still decay naturally and that
      low-string stops remain clean.

  - title: "Try optional Flangerish"
    description: >-
      Place Flangerish after the cabinet in this simple serial chain.
      As conservative, ear-led starting positions, try Rate around 2/10,
      Depth around 2/10 and Feedback at 0-1/10. If the block exposes a
      Mix or Blend control, begin around 1-2/10. These are practical
      proposals, not researched settings. Listen for a slow, shifting
      movement behind the riff while the pick attack and low C stay
      clear. Bypass it or reduce Depth/Mix if the sweep becomes obvious
      or softens the attack.

  - id: "B"
    name: "Heavy Rhythm"
    description: >-
      Practical proposal: keep the same serial chain and set amp Gain

      output level with Scene A; do not rely on extra loudness for the
      heavier impression. These are not verified recording Scene or
      section-specific settings.

troubleshooting:
  - problem: "Low C sounds loose or muddy?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Bass slightly, check the guitar's string tension and
      muting, then try Green 808 with low Drive and higher Level.
      Avoid raising the gate threshold so far that note tails are cut.

  - problem: "The tone is too thin or sharp?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Presence or Treble slightly and check the cabinet
      microphone position. If using Green 808, lower its Tone before
      adding more amp gain.

  - problem: "The guitar disappears in the mix?"
    solution: >-
      Add a little Mid on Brit 2203 and compare at matched volume.
      Keep enough mids for the riff to remain clear alongside bass
      and drums.

  - problem: "The sound is too compressed?"
    solution: >-
      Bypass Green 808 or reduce its Level, then lower amp Gain
      slightly. Let the amplifier provide the main distortion.

  - problem: "Noise is cut off too abruptly?"
    solution: >-
      Lower the Simple Gate threshold or bypass it. Keep the gate
      strong enough to control pauses but allow sustained notes to
      decay naturally.

  - problem: "Flangerish sounds too obvious or blurs the riff?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Depth and any Mix/Blend setting first, then lower Feedback
      or bypass Flangerish. Keep the rate slow and listen at matched
      volume; the goal is subtle movement behind the notes while the
      pick attack and low C remain clear.

  - problem: "The guitar still lacks the song's swirling character?"
    solution: >-
      Engage optional Flangerish after the cabinet and raise Depth or
      Mix/Blend slightly at a time while keeping the rate slow. Treat
      this as an ear-led approximation, not a reconstruction of the
      unverified original effect.
