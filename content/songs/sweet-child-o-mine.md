beginner_route: >-

veteran_notes: >-
  Slash has said that he recorded Appetite for Destruction with a
  handmade 1959 Les Paul copy built by Kris Derrig. He also recalled
  that the solo tone on Sweet Child O' Mine came from a Marshall, while
  the clean verse chords used a Roland Jazz Chorus 120. Accounts of the
  Marshall used on Appetite differ: Slash has called it a JCM800, other
  research identifies a 1959 Super Lead or 1959T Tremolo, and Marshall
  has suggested a pre-JCM800 2203. The rental amp was modified for an
  extra gain stage, so no stock Quad Cortex amp is an exact match.

  Brit 2203 is based on a Marshall JCM800 and is the selected
  approximation for the hot-rodded Marshall character. Brit Plexi 100
  Bright is another verified option if you prefer the Super Lead
  family as a starting point. Neither model recreates the missing
  modified rental amplifier. The 412 Brit 60B GB 90s cabinet is a
  practical Marshall-style cabinet choice; the exact cabinet and
  speaker combination for every track overdub is not established here.

  Slash's Derrig Les Paul is documented, but exact pickup selection
  and pickup position can vary across overdubs and are not confirmed
  for each part of this song. Begin on the bridge humbucker for a
  defined attack. Try the neck humbucker if you want a rounder,
  singing lead sound.

  Slash said he used a stock Cry Baby during the Appetite for
  Destruction period, and the song's lead solo is associated with a
  wah sound. Crying Wah is the Quad Cortex's verified Cry Baby
  GCB-95-based model. Its use and settings here are practical
  approximations, not recovered studio automation or pedal positions.

  Slash recalled using a Roland JC-120 for the clean verse chords.
  The Quad Cortex includes Rols Jazz CH120, based on that amplifier.
  That clean sound is intentionally not drawn in this serial lead
  chain. To cover the verse, build a separate clean preset using
  Rols Jazz CH120 and a suitable Roland-style cabinet model.

before_you_build: >-
  Tune to E-flat standard: Eb-Ab-Db-Gb-Bb-Eb, from the lowest string
  to the highest. This is one semitone below standard tuning and is
  needed to match the original recording's pitch and fret positions.

  A Les Paul-style guitar with humbuckers is the closest practical
  starting point to Slash's documented Appetite-era guitar. Use the
  bridge pickup first for the riff and a cutting lead sound. If the
  lead becomes too sharp, try the neck pickup or roll the guitar tone
  control down slightly.

  Keep the guitar volume and tone controls open while setting the
  initial amp sound. Use a moderate pick attack for the repeating
  arpeggios, then dig in for bends and sustained lead notes.

build_steps:
  - title: "Prepare the guitar"
    description: >-
      Tune to E-flat standard (Eb-Ab-Db-Gb-Bb-Eb). Start with a
      Les Paul-style guitar and bridge humbucker. Confirm that the
      strings remain in tune during wide bends, and set your guitar
      volume fully open while dialling in the amp.

  - title: "Set the Marshall-style amplifier"
    description: >-
      Select Brit 2203. As approximate starting positions, try Gain
      around 5.5/10, Bass 4.5/10, Mid 6/10, Treble 5.5/10 and
      Presence 4.5/10, where those controls are available. Adjust the
      output or master level for the required volume. The source amp
      was modified and its exact settings are unknown, so use these
      values as starting points rather than historical facts.

  - title: "Choose the cabinet and microphone"
    description: >-
      Select 412 Brit 60B GB 90s. If the cab block offers microphone
      choices, start with a dynamic microphone slightly off the
      speaker centre. If low and high cuts are available, try
      approximately 80 Hz and 8 kHz, then adjust by ear. Reduce the
      high cut if the tone is dull or move the microphone farther
      towards the cone edge if the top end is harsh.

  - title: "Set the Cry Baby-style wah"
    description: >-
      Add Crying Wah before the amp. Leave it bypassed while setting
      the riff tone. For the lead, connect an expression pedal if
      available and sweep gradually through the range rather than
      parking at the toe position. If the effect sounds too nasal,
      reduce the sweep range or use less toe travel. These are
      practical adjustments, not verified settings of Slash's
      original stock Cry Baby.

  - title: "Balance the lead level"
    description: >-
      Compare the riff and solo scenes at performance volume. If the
      lead needs to sit forward, raise its scene output by roughly
      1 to 2 dB before adding more amp gain. Keep the notes defined
      and the bends vocal; excessive gain can blur the riff's
      string-to-string detail.

scenes:
  - id: "A"
    name: "Main Riff"
    description: >-
      Use Brit 2203 and 412 Brit 60B GB 90s with the suggested
      starting settings. Bypass Crying Wah. Keep enough gain for a
      full, singing sound while preserving the articulation of the
      repeating arpeggios.

  - id: "B"
    name: "Wah Lead"
    description: >-
      Keep the same amp and cabinet. Engage Crying Wah and use a
      broad, expressive sweep for the solo. Raise the scene output
      by about 1 to 2 dB if the lead needs more presence. Add only
      a small amount of amp gain if extra sustain is needed.

troubleshooting:
  - problem: "The riff sounds muddy?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Bass slightly, check that the guitar is tuned to
      E-flat standard, and try the bridge humbucker. If needed,
      raise the cabinet low cut a little from its starting point.

  - problem: "The lead sounds too harsh?"
    solution: >-
      Reduce Presence or Treble slightly, move the cabinet
      microphone farther from the speaker centre if available,
      or try the neck pickup for a rounder lead tone.

  - problem: "There is not enough sustain?"
    solution: >-
      Raise Brit 2203 Gain gradually and check that the scene output
      is not too low. Increase gain only until the notes sustain
      through bends; preserve enough pick definition for the riff.

  - problem: "The wah sounds too pronounced?"
    solution: >-
      Sweep through a smaller portion of the Crying Wah range and
      avoid holding it fully at the toe position. Bypass it for the
      main riff and engage it only for the lead part.
